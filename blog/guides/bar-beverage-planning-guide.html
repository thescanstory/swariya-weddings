Bar & Beverage Planning

Alcohol selection, bar setup, and beverage costs. Expert guide by Swariya Weddings.

Bride and groom laughing together at night — from a Swariya-planned wedding

Alcohol selection, bar setup, and beverage costs. Expert guide by Swariya Weddings. Here's how our planning team approaches it, drawn from 150+ weddings across Bengaluru, Mysuru and Hyderabad.

Where to start

Most couples come to this decision later than they should — not because it isn't important, but because it's easy to assume it will sort itself out once the bigger pieces (venue, date, guest count) are locked. In practice, bar & beverage planning works better as an early conversation, not an afterthought squeezed in during the final weeks.

Start by naming what you actually want, in plain terms, before looking at options. It's tempting to scroll inspiration first — but a clear brief, even a rough one, makes every vendor conversation shorter and every decision easier to defend later.

"The couples who feel calmest on their wedding day are rarely the ones with the biggest budget — they're the ones who decided early, and didn't revisit the decision five times."

The planning sequence

01

Clarify the vision

Before vendors or venues, agree on what matters most to you both about bar & beverage planning — this becomes the filter for every decision that follows.

02

Set the budget

Assign real numbers to each line item early, with a contingency of 10–15% held back for the details you can't predict yet.

03

Build the shortlist

Narrow vendors and options to a small, serious shortlist — three to five — rather than comparing everything on offer.

04

Confirm and coordinate

Lock decisions in writing, build a shared timeline, and let one point of contact carry it through to the day itself.

What tends to go wrong

  • Deciding under time pressure, in the last month, when options have thinned out
  • Choosing based on a single photo or reel rather than a full portfolio or site visit
  • Leaving verbal agreements unconfirmed in writing
  • Not building in a buffer for the one thing that always shifts

A note from our planning team

We treat bar & beverage planning as one part of a larger picture, not an isolated checklist item — because a choice made here almost always affects three or four other decisions downstream, from timeline to budget to guest experience. That's the part a vendor comparison site won't tell you, and it's usually where an experienced planner earns their place.

Bringing it together

None of this needs to be complicated. The couples who navigate it well tend to make fewer decisions, more deliberately, and lean on people who've done it before to catch what they can't yet see. That's the whole idea behind how we plan.
