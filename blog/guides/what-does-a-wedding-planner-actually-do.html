What Does a Wedding Planner Actually Do?

A concrete breakdown of what a wedding planner handles day-to-day, from the first consultation through the wedding day itself.

A wedding ceremony held at sunset — from a Swariya-planned wedding

The job is easy to underestimate from the outside — it looks like picking flowers and sending a few emails. In practice it's closer to running a small, one-time project with dozens of moving parts and a fixed, non-negotiable deadline.

Before the wedding

  • Understanding your vision, budget, and non-negotiables in the first consultation
  • Shortlisting and negotiating with venues based on your actual guest count and style
  • Vetting and coordinating vendors — catering, décor, photography, entertainment
  • Building and holding the master timeline everyone else works against

On the wedding day

  • Being the single point of contact so vendors aren't calling you or your family directly
  • Managing setup, run-of-show timing, and troubleshooting the small things that inevitably shift
  • Handling the one problem that always comes up, quietly, before you even hear about it

What a planner does not replace

Your own decisions on style, priorities, and budget — a good planner narrows options and executes, but the wedding should still feel like yours, not a template applied to your date.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do wedding planners also do the decor themselves?

Some do; many coordinate specialist décor vendors instead. Worth asking directly what's in-house versus subcontracted when comparing planners.

Will I still need to make a lot of decisions myself?

Yes — a planner narrows and executes options, but taste, budget priorities, and final sign-off stay with you throughout.
