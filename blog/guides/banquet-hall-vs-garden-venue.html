Banquet Hall vs. Garden Venue

Comparing indoor banquet halls and outdoor garden venues for Bengaluru weddings — cost, weather risk, and guest experience.

A garden aisle set for the ceremony — from a Swariya-planned wedding

This is usually the first real fork in the road after guest count is settled, and it shapes almost every decision that follows — décor, catering setup, even what month makes sense.

Banquet hall: the case for it

Weather-proof by default, usually comes with in-house catering and basic décor infrastructure (stage, lighting rig) already in place, and climate control matters more in Bengaluru's warmer months than people expect. Setup and teardown logistics are simpler because everything happens in one controlled space.

Garden venue: the case for it

Natural light transforms photography, and the open-air feel is hard to replicate indoors no matter how good the décor. The tradeoff is real weather dependency — even Bengaluru's relatively mild climate needs a genuine covered backup plan, not a token one, especially outside peak season.

The real deciding factor

It's rarely aesthetics alone — it's your date. A garden venue booked for December is a very different risk profile than one booked for July.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are garden venues more expensive than banquet halls?

Not inherently — pricing depends more on the specific property and season than the venue type itself. Premium banquet halls can cost more than modest garden venues and vice versa.

What's a realistic backup plan for a garden wedding?

A covered structure (tent or existing pavilion) sized for your full guest count, not a partial one — a half-measure backup plan is often worse than no backup plan, since it creates false confidence.
